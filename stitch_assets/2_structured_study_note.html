Cellular Respiration: Glycolysis & Krebs Cycle

Professor Vance • Science Hall Rm 302 • Transcribed with AI Vision & Audio

auto_awesome

Executive AI Digest

Key Takeaways
  • Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm, is anaerobic, and converts 1 Glucose into 2 Pyruvate yielding net 2 ATP & 2 NADH.
  • Link Reaction transports pyruvate into mitochondrial matrix, releasing CO₂ to create Acetyl-CoA.
  • Krebs (Citric Acid) Cycle processes Acetyl-CoA through an 8-step catalytic cycle producing 3 NADH, 1 FADH₂, 1 GTP/ATP, and 2 CO₂ per turn (doubled per glucose).
  • Key Exam Warning: Remember that oxygen is not directly required for the Krebs cycle itself, but cycle halts without O₂ due to depletion of NAD+ / FAD from the ETC!

Detailed Breakdown

3 Sections
1Glycolysis Pathway (Cytosol)
expand_more

Glycolysis consists of an energy investment phase followed by an energy payoff phase:

C₆H₁₂O₆ + 2 NAD⁺ + 2 ADP + 2 Pᵢ → 2 C₃H₄O₃ + 2 NADH + 2 H⁺ + 2 ATP + 2 H₂O
Investment Phase-2 ATP consumed (Hexokinase & PFK-1)
Payoff Phase+4 ATP & +2 NADH (Net +2 ATP)
2The Krebs (Citric Acid) Cycle
expand_more

Oxaloacetate (4C) binds Acetyl-CoA (2C) through Citrate Synthase to form Citrate (6C). Through successive decarboxylations and oxidations:

  • Rate Limiting Enzyme: Isocitrate Dehydrogenase (inhibited by high ATP/NADH).
  • FADH₂ Generation: Succinate Dehydrogenase (complex II embedded in inner mitochondrial membrane).
  • Regeneration: Malate dehydrogenase regenerates Oxaloacetate to continue the cycle.
3Oxidative Phosphorylation Bridge
expand_more

Electrons carried by NADH and FADH₂ are transferred through protein complexes I-IV, creating a proton gradient across the inner mitochondrial membrane driving ATP Synthase via chemiosmosis.
